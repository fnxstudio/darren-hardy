A. The curveSmallest change

DH's layout as is, but the rule stops being a line and becomes the compound curve: flat for most of its run, then it turns and climbs, with the glint at the tip. It says "compound" without a word, and it works at any size, even as a favicon or a lapel pin.

B. The pennyThe plug-in-the-D move

The O in FOUNDERS is a minted coin: the magic penny from the original book, the story every diehard TCE fan already knows. The curve is engraved on its face. This is the direct equivalent of the plug: one letter becomes the idea, and the coin can stand alone as the event's mark (stage, challenge coin, wax seal, social avatar).

C. The cornerstoneMost "real"

Founders lay the foundation, so the logo is an engraved, bolted steel plate, like the dedication plaque on a building. Type is cut into the metal instead of floating on black. Nothing reads more like a real object, and it photographs well printed or etched for the in-studio guests.

D. The serial plateLuxury launch cue

Borrowed from the limited-edition car plate (the Ferrari launch reference DH liked): every ticket gets its own number, so the logo carries "Founder Nº 001" on the badge, ticket, certificate and confirmation page. It makes "founder" a role you hold, not a word on a banner, and it ties straight to the Founder 100.

E. Red, like the jacketTies it to the book

The jacket sets IN BUSINESS in red, so the logo does too, in brushed red metal, with the curve in the same red. One look at the book and one look at the logo and they are obviously the same family.
