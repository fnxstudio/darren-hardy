Founders Event: logo sketches

Flat type first, the way a concept gets judged before the finish goes on. The brushed metal comes last, rendered properly with a horizon line, bevel and restrained highlights, on whichever sketch DH picks. Red is shown where the accent would go.

A. The rising VLetter carries the idea

The V in EVENT keeps its thick left stroke. Its thin right stroke becomes the compound curve: a hairline that starts slow, grows heavier and climbs past the cap height. The word still reads EVENT, and the curve lives inside a letter instead of sitting beside the type. That's the rule behind the plug in the D: swap a simple middle letter, never the first or last.

B. The compounding lineDivider with running room

The rule between the two lines becomes the compounding line. It starts under the F as a hairline, runs flat across almost the whole word, then thickens and climbs past the end of the top line. Most of the run is flat on purpose, because that is what compounding looks like before it pays off.

C. The inscriptionFounding stone

Roman inscriptional capitals are the letters cut into dedication stones, so a founding event gets founding typography. Two period details are real: words split by a triangular interpunct, and numerals marked with a bar over the top (MMXXVII). The Romans painted red into the cut letters, which gives the red accent a reason. These letters also carry less stroke contrast than DH's Didone serif, so they hold a metal bevel better when rendered.

Stand-insFor the sketch only

A and B use Playfair Display in place of DH's serif, and C uses Cinzel for the inscriptional capitals. Both are placeholders for the final faces. The top line uses Bebas Neue, close to the jacket's Bebas Neue Pro.
