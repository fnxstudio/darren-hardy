Thirty-one.

A penny that doubles every month is worth $5.12 at month ten. It is worth $5,242.88 at month twenty. At month thirty-one it is worth $10,737,418.24.

Almost all of it arrives in the final few months. Long after any reasonable person would have quit.

You know that law. You have been running it on yourself since the first book. Here is what I did not understand when I wrote that one.

In a business, the unit of compounding is not one person's behavior. It is every behavior, running through five multipliers at once: how often it repeats, how many people carry it, how many handoffs spread it, how many customers feel it, and how long it has been running.

The penny doubles. An org chart multiplies.

Which means your company is compounding this week, in a direction you did not choose and are probably not watching. Faster than you compound personally. Less forgiving in both directions.

And it will not tell you. That is the part that costs leaders everything.

Consequences in business run a year or more behind behavior. Bezos said a good quarter was fully baked about three years ago. So a company that is eroding still shows you last year's momentum, and a company that is building shows you nothing at all for months. Both of them look sudden at month thirty-one.

Here is the sentence I would put on the inside of your office door.

You're always thirty-one months from becoming unrecognizable. In either direction.

Not someday. Starting now. Thirty-one months from this morning is May 2029.

I want to be careful here, because you are not a leader in trouble. If you were, you would already be moving. You are something more dangerous. You are a leader whose numbers look fine.

If your business is good right now, this is more urgent for you, not less. Decline announces itself eventually. Flat never does.
