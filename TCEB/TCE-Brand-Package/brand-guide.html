One system for the franchise. The Compound Effect (2010) and The Compound Effect in Business (April 2027) are not two brands. They are one brand printed on opposite grounds, and almost everything in this document follows from that.

02 · The franchise

One brand,
printed two ways.

The 2010 jacket is red and black on white. The 2027 jacket is white and red on black. Measured side by side, the two reds sit within a few points of each other. The books were never two identities. They are an inversion of one.

The rule that falls out of it. A white book needs a dark ground to exist against. A black book needs a light one. So TCE artwork goes on dark, TCEB artwork goes on light, and any page showing both has to give each its own field rather than splitting the difference.
Inversion is not a palette swap. When a treatment moves between the two grounds, invert the physics, not the colours. A glow becomes a shadow. A vignette that lightens becomes one that darkens. An object that separates by emitting becomes one that separates by casting. Swapping hex values alone produces the effect that looks correct in a spec and wrong on a screen.
03 · Identity

Three layers,
in this order.

The author, the franchise, the book. Every page carries at least the first two, and they are never given equal weight in the same view.

1 · Darren Hardy

The author mark. This is his brand before it is a book brand, and the franchise sits under it. Used in the byline position on a jacket, and in the footer on the web. It is never the primary mark on a book page and never competes with the title lockup.

Set in Franklin Gothic. His name is the one place that face appears. Using it anywhere else spends the only thing that makes the byline distinct.

2 · The Compound Effect

The franchise lockup: THE / COMPOUND / EFFECT stacked in the display face, with a red rule beneath. This is the parent form and it is identical on both books.

It carries the equity of two million copies. It is the constant, and the reason the second book is recognised at all.

3 · The tail

Each book extends the parent lockup rather than replacing it. The 2010 book adds nothing. The 2027 book adds IN BUSINESS, set smaller than EFFECT, in red, on its own line. A future title would take the same position at the same relative size.

NoThe tail set at the same size as EFFECT. It is a qualifier, not a third word of the title.
NoThe franchise lockup and the DH mark at matching weight in one view. One leads.
NoFranklin Gothic anywhere except his name.
NoThe red rule omitted. It is part of the lockup, not decoration around it.
YesLockup centred, its own clear space of at least the cap-height of COMPOUND on all four sides.
Open · needs DH

The lockup is currently type set in the display face rather than a drawn mark. For a four-site property that is worth revisiting: a drawn wordmark survives at small sizes, in one colour, and on merchandise in a way that set type does not. Flagged rather than assumed.

04 · The grounds

Two grounds.
Nothing between them.

The jacket does not use a tinted paper, a cream, or a warm grey. It is pure white against pure black, and the hardness of that edge is the identity.

White
#FFFFFF
Flaps, back panel, the bands that frame the cover. Where the argument is made and the reading happens.
Black
#000000
The cover itself. Where the title lives, and the only ground gold is permitted on.
This corrects the earlier proposal. A warm paper at #fbfaf8 and a mist grey at #dcd9d5 were drafted before the jacket was available. They soften a system that is meant to be hard, and they cost contrast: the red measures 4.64:1 on warm paper, which fails body text, and 4.85:1 on pure white, which passes. The jacket is both more elegant and more accessible.
05 · The accent

One red. Once.

On the jacket the red appears on a single phrase per surface. IN BUSINESS on the cover. One sentence on the flap. One word in the URL. That discipline is the reason it lands.

Red
#E1132E
The accent on white. Headlines, one emphasis, and button fills with white type.
4.85:1 on white · passes
Red bright
#FF0047
The same role on black. Type only. Never a button fill: white on it is 3.93.
5.34:1 on black · passes
Gold
#D4A017
Founding tiers only, and only on dark. Gold fills carry black type, never white.
8.84:1 on black · 2.38 on white
Gold lux
#CAA76A
The softer register, for when the tier should read premium rather than loud.
9.25:1 on black · 2.27 on white
Each red passes on exactly one ground. #E1132E is 4.85:1 on white and 4.33:1 on black. #FF0047 is 5.34:1 on black and 3.93:1 on white. So they are not interchangeable and neither is a "lighter version" of the other: white surfaces take #E1132E, black surfaces take #FF0047, and swapping them fails contrast in both directions.
NoRed on red. Red headline above a red button is two shouts, not one.
NoGold anywhere a light ground is in play. #D4A017 is 2.38:1 on white.
NoGold type set beside red type at the same size. They are only 1.65:1 apart in luminance, so they vibrate. Separate them by size, weight or space.
NoA second accent hue. There is not one, and adding one breaks the jacket.
YesLong stretches with no colour at all. That is what makes the red work.

07 · The faces

Confirmed from
the jacket.

Two of the three are now settled by DH. The title is Bebas Neue Pro. His name is Franklin Gothic. Nothing here is an approximation of the cover any more.

Display · the title
Bebas Neue

Headlines, eyebrows, moneylines, tier names, any standalone number. Always uppercase, which is free because the face has no lowercase.

Jacket sets Bebas Neue Pro (commercial, Fontfabric). Self-hosted here is free Bebas Neue, the single-weight cut of the same family. Oswald remains the fallback.

The name · condensed
Darren Hardy

His name only, wherever it is set large: the cover band, a page footer, a signature. It is deliberately not the display face, which is what makes the byline read as a separate voice.

Jacket sets Franklin Gothic Condensed (commercial, ITC/Monotype). That cut has no OFL equivalent, so Archivo Narrow stands in here, the closest free face in the American-gothic family. The real name is first in the stack, so a licensed machine uses it.

Body
Inter

Every paragraph, lede, caption, form field, button and piece of fine print. If a person reads more than one line of it, it is Inter.

NoNever ask Bebas for a bold. The free cut ships one weight, so 500 or 700 makes the browser synthesise a fake bold and smear the letterforms. Weight stays 400 and emphasis comes from size, which is how the jacket builds its hierarchy anyway.
NoNever set a paragraph in Bebas. It is a display face with no lowercase.
NoNever set a headline in Inter or Franklin. Those read as a web app and a byline respectively.
NoNever italicise. The system has no italic: emphasis is the accent colour, which is why em is redefined as red rather than slanted.
YesFranklin is reserved for his name. Using it elsewhere spends the one thing that makes the byline distinct.
Two licences to price before production. What is self-hosted today is free and safe to ship, but it is not what the jacket is set in. Bebas Neue Pro adds the Book, Bold and Expanded cuts plus lowercase; if the printed title uses any of those, the site and the book will visibly diverge until a webfont licence is bought. Franklin Gothic is the same situation: Libre Franklin is a good revival, but set at cover size next to the real thing the difference shows.
Still open: the body face on the jacket. DH confirmed the title and the name. The flap and back-panel copy is set in a condensed gothic that is plausibly Franklin, not Inter. If it is, there is a case for moving web body copy to Franklin too and running the whole property on two faces instead of three. Worth confirming before the type system is locked.
08 · Licence & hosting

Buy the real faces.
Self-host both.

DH confirmed the jacket faces. Both are commercial, and what is running on the web pages today is free approximations of them.

Face
Position
Bebas Neue Pro
Display. Titles, lockups, headlines, numbers. Licence required. Currently substituted with Bebas Neue (free), which lacks the weights and the lowercase.
Franklin Gothic
The author name only. Licence required. Currently substituted with Archivo Narrow.
Body face
Long-form reading. Not yet confirmed against the jacket, and possibly Franklin, which would make this a two-face system rather than three.
Self-host, never a CDN. Both faces are served as woff2 from the property itself via @font-face. No Google Fonts, no third-party font host. That keeps the licence auditable, removes a render-blocking third party, and stops the type changing under the brand if a CDN changes what it serves.
Open · blocking

Neither licence is purchased. Until they are, every page is running approximations and the web property does not match the printed jacket. This is the one open item that blocks shipping a site rather than just a document.

The caps ceiling. Display is always uppercase, and uppercase is capped by word count, not by line count: eight words maximum, six preferred. 11 · Buttons & the CTA system

One primary
per view.

Two tiers, and they never appear at the same weight in the same screen. The ghost invites. The solid converts.

Primary · solid
Get on the list →

Red fill, white type, 3px radius. One per view, at the point of conversion. On a dark ground the fill stays red; it does not become white.

Secondary · ghost
See the book

Transparent, 1px border, type in the ground's ink. Used in the nav and beside a primary. It may fill on hover; the solid may not.

NoA solid button never darkens on hover. Colour is held constant. It grows and the arrow travels.
NoPill shapes. 3px radius everywhere. The jacket has no round corners and neither does this.
NoTwo solids in one view. If a second action is needed it is a ghost or a link.
YesGhost may fill on hover, flipping its type to the ground colour.
YesPlacement: ghost in the nav, content between, solid at the close.
Why the hover rule exists. Darkening on hover reads as the button being spent or disabled. Growing and moving the arrow reads as the button responding. The colour is a brand constant and hover is not the place to modulate it.
12 · Forms & the glass card

A lens,
not a pane.

Frosted glass is one layer: blur plus a milky veil. It hides what is behind it and reads flat. The brand's form card is a lens. The body is barely blurred and all the character lives at the rim, where the light bends.

Layer
Job
frost
The legibility blur. Always on, and deliberately separate from the refraction so a browser that cannot render the bend still produces a readable form.
refract
The bend itself: a displacement filter masked to the rim only. Refraction belongs in the bevel; the centre of a slab passes light straight through.
lens
A second, harder filter faded inward over the rim. A hard-edged mask here reads as a picture frame.
fringe
Chromatic dispersion, kept under 10% or it stops reading as glass and starts reading as a mistake.
edge
Every bevel and inset highlight. Above the glass, or its own blur softens the 1px lit edge.
spec
A highlight travelling around the rim, driven by an animated conic angle.
The trap that silently kills it. An element with backdrop-filter, isolation:isolate, or will-change:transform becomes a backdrop root. A descendant's backdrop-filter can then only see what is painted inside that root, which is nothing. The card paints, the glass does not, and there is no error. If the glass is missing, look for a backdrop root above it before looking at the filter.
On a light ground, commit to black. A dark card at 20–30% over a photograph is not glass, it is dirt: the plate shows through as grey and the eyebrow drops to around 3:1. The light version is a solid near-black slab that keeps the rim, the bevel and the specular, and gives up the transparency that was buying nothing.
13 · Nav & footers

Two footers.
Pick deliberately.

The jacket's device carries straight to the page: a band above, a band below, and the statement between them. The nav is the top band. The footer is the bottom one.

Slim footer

One line: identity, a legal link, nothing else. Every action page takes this — sign-up, sale lead-in, confirmation. A page whose job is one decision does not offer twelve other ways to leave.

Full footer

Navigation, secondary links, the DH parent mark. General brand pages only. If you are unsure which a page is, it is an action page and it takes the slim one.

NoFull footer on a confirmation page.
NoA solid CTA in the nav. The nav invites; it does not convert.
YesNav carries the franchise lockup at small size, and one ghost action, right-aligned.
YesThe footer is where the DH parent mark lives.
14 · Testimonials & imagery

The jacket is
the only asset.

This property owns two pieces of real imagery: the two jackets. Everything else is borrowed, commissioned, or invented, and the rules differ sharply between those.

NoA testimonial is a banked result. Already happened, with a magnitude. A projection, a compliment or an event story is not a testimonial, and a bigger number does not rescue a projection.
NoCircular avatars. A rectangular portrait beside the quote, in a split layout.
NoCut-outs on a brand-colour field. Real photo on its real background. Colour lives in the card, never behind the head.
NoA subject whose body is turned away from the copy. Fix by side or by mirror, and never mirror branded kit.
NoGenerated imagery of people or places. Retouching a real photograph is fine; generating one is not.
Separating a black book from a dark page. The jacket is the darkest thing in the frame, so it cannot be separated by value. It is separated by light: an ambient pool behind it, a rim along the lit edge, and a real contact shadow beneath. That is how black product is photographed and it is the answer to "the jacket is black so the page cannot be".
Give it thickness. A flat image of a cover reads as a picture of a book. A spine, a page edge and a floor read as a book. Where the object is the subject, build it.

16 · Web implementation

The tokens.
Use these, not new ones.

Paste block. Anything that has to sit on the ground must read a token that moves with the ground, never a hardcoded value.

:root{
  /* grounds */
  --c-white:#ffffff;   --c-black:#000000;   --c-near:#0e0d10;
  --c-paper:#fbfaf8;   --c-bone:#f0f0f0;    --c-ink:#241f21;

  /* accent. red once per view. gold on dark grounds only. */
  --c-red:#e1132e;     --c-red-bright:#ff0047;
  --c-gold:#d4a017;    --c-gold-lux:#caa76a;

  /* neutrals are type tiers, not grounds */
  --fg:#000;  --fg-soft:#4d4d4d;  --fg-mute:#6e6e6e;  --fg-fine:#b3b3b3;

  /* type */
  --font-display:'Bebas Neue Pro',sans-serif;
  --font-name:'Franklin Gothic Condensed',sans-serif;
  --font-body:system-ui,sans-serif;

  /* geometry. 3px everywhere. no pills. */
  --radius:3px;  --container:1200px;  --gutter:clamp(20px,4vw,56px);
}
The token trap. A value written as a literal inside a rule that also serves the other theme will be correct on one ground and wrong on the other, and it will not error. Vignettes, scrims, footers and anything inverted are where this happens. If it sits on the ground, it reads a token.

18 · Word craft

How it is written.

The type rules and the copy rules are one system. A headline that breaks these will look wrong no matter how it is set.

NoEm dashes. Ever. Restructure the sentence.
No"Buy." They invest. They never purchase.
NoOpening on the problem. Lead with the outcome and its emotion. Format, duration and mechanics never lead.
NoAn all-caps headline over eight words. Six is better. Split a longer idea into a caps punch plus sentence-case detail.
NoNaming the paid product on a free or pre-sale page, testimonials included. Trim the quote instead.
NoFunnel language anywhere a reader can see it, page names included.
Yes"Leader", not "owner".
YesAfter a purchase, affirm the decision first and offer the multiplier second. No loss framing.
Orphans are a type failure, not a copy failure. The display face is set in authored line breaks, not wrapped. A single word alone on a line is the most common way to make this face look cheap. Size the type to the break, do not narrow the measure until the break appears.
