Publish a week of Sessions in five minutes, not a full day.

This is the step-by-step for loading DarrenDaily sessions into the website. No code, no design skills, no Webflow experience needed. If you can fill in a spreadsheet, you can run this.

The 60-second version

What this replaces

Every DarrenDaily session used to be built by hand, one at a time, inside the website builder: create the page, paste the title, the caption, the quote, upload and optimize the thumbnail, set the dates, add the CTA. Twenty-something fields per session, every single weekday.

Now you fill one row in a spreadsheet per session, hand the sheet to Claude, and it builds every page for you in one pass. Then it hands you a list of links to spot-check. That's the whole job.

The flow, start to finish
1 · Fill the sheet → 2 · Paste it to Claude → 3 · Review the links → 4 · Publish
Why we do it this way

Step 1 · One-time setup

Get connected (you only do this once)

Before your first batch, three things need to be true. After that, you skip straight to Step 2 every time.

A

Open Claude

You can use either one, whichever you already have open:

  • Claude for desktop or web (Cowork) — the regular Claude app or claude.ai. Easiest for most people.
  • Claude Code — the terminal version, if that's how your team works.

Both work identically for this job. Nothing here needs the Webflow Designer to be open, and you never touch code.

B

Connect the Webflow connector

This is the bridge that lets Claude build pages on our site. In Claude's settings, open Connectors, find Webflow, and authorize it against the Darren Hardy LLC workspace. Once it shows connected, you're set.

If it's not connected

Claude will stop and tell you it can't reach Webflow. Just go back to Connectors and authorize it. Don't guess your way around it, and never paste passwords into the chat.

C

Have the two files handy

Keep these two together. You'll download the first one right below.

  • The intake spreadsheet — one row per session (download it in Step 2).
  • The bulk instructions file — Sessions-Claude-Bulk-Instructions.md. You hand this to Claude alongside the sheet so it knows exactly what to do. Ask your team lead for it if you don't have it.
Step 2 · Fill the spreadsheet

One row per session

Download the template, open it in Excel or Google Sheets, and add one row for each session you want to publish. The two example rows at the top are real, already-published sessions — leave them as a reference or delete them before you send.

Downloads Sessions-Intake-Template.csv straight to your computer. Opens in Excel or Google Sheets.

The fast path: usually just two columns

If the session already lives on dd.darrenhardy.com, you often only need to fill two cells. Claude pulls everything else — title, video, caption, quote, thumbnail — straight from that page.

No source page? (brand-new session)

Then fill the three required columns yourself: Name, Published Date (as 2026-07-28), and Vimeo ID (the number from vimeo.com/1210284296). Add the caption, quote, and meta description in their columns.

What each column is for

ColumnWhat to put there
NameThe session title, exactly as it should read.
Published DateYYYY-MM-DD. Drives the date on the page, the sort order, and the 72-hour expiry.
Vimeo IDThe number only, e.g. 1210284296.
CaptionThe comment prompt under the video. Wrap the lead-in in **double asterisks** for bold.
Secondary CaptionThe follow-up / referral copy under the caption.
Moneyline Quote + AttributionThe pull-quote for the right column, and who said it.
Meta Description~155 characters for search and social previews.
Featured CTA IDThe HubSpot CTA code for the right-column "Refer an A-Player" card. One per session. Blank = the default.
Banner columnsOptional per-session banners under the video or pinned on top. Leave blank to use site defaults.
AUTO columnsSlug, thumbnail, social image, SEO title, expire date — leave blank. Filled for you.
Images: you don't do anything

Paste any image as a public link (whatever format you have). Every image that shows on the page is automatically converted to fast, optimized WebP on upload — the thumbnail, any banners, all of it. The one exception is the Social Share image, which is deliberately left as PNG/JPG because some Facebook and LinkedIn previews break on WebP. This is handled for you; it's here just so you know why one image looks different.

Step 3 · Run the build

Hand it to Claude

Open Claude, attach or paste your filled spreadsheet and the bulk instructions file, and send this message. That's the entire ask — Claude does the rest.

Paste thisHere is our filled Sessions intake sheet and the bulk-build instructions. Please build each row into the DarrenDaily Sessions CMS in Webflow, following the instructions file exactly. Work in drafts, convert every on-page image to WebP, then show me the full list of live session URLs for review when you're done.

What Claude is doing for you

  • Reads each row and pulls anything missing from the source page.
  • Creates the session page, fills every field, sets the date and the 72-hour expiry.
  • Uploads each on-page image and converts it to WebP automatically.
  • Drops in the right CTA for each session.
  • Keeps going if one row has a problem, and flags it for you instead of stopping.
The 30-day batch window

You can safely load up to about 30 days of sessions ahead in a single batch. Each one reveals itself on its Published date and drops off the feeds 72 hours later, all on its own — no plugin, no babysitting. Load the batch, publish once (Step 5), and walk away; the site rotates day by day for the whole window. Keep each batch inside ~30 days so the earliest upcoming post doesn't get pushed out of the "latest sessions" window.

Step 4 · Review

Check the links it hands back

The moment the batch finishes, Claude posts a table of every session it built, with a clickable link for each one, right there in the chat. Open each, give it a look, done. Anything that needed a fallback or hit a snag is flagged with the reason.

If a task manager is connected

If something like Asana is connected to your Claude session, it can also push a review task containing those links straight to whoever does the checking — so the reviewer gets pinged instead of relying on someone spotting the chat. Just ask for it in the same message. If no task manager is connected, the links in the chat are your list.

Step 5 · Publish

Push it live

Claude publishes the individual session items for you. To make brand-new pages and the cross-page "latest sessions" feed refresh across the whole site, one human still clicks the big Publish in Webflow.

1

Claude publishes the items

Each session it built gets published as it goes, so the pages exist and are live.

2

You do one full-site Publish

Open Webflow and hit Publish (top-right). This refreshes the "latest sessions" strips on the Welcome and Expired pages and makes any brand-new page fully live. You only need to do this once per batch.

Good to know

After that single publish, the site self-rotates for the whole batch window — future sessions appear on their day and expired ones fall off automatically. You do not re-publish to reveal or expire sessions; you only publish again when you load the next batch.

Step 6 · Page expiry

Making old session pages actually close (JetBoost)

Two different things are going on with expiry, and it's worth keeping them straight:

1 · The feeds hide the link automatically. After 72 hours a session drops off every list, feed, and exit-popup on its own, driven by the dates in the sheet. Nothing to set up — this is already built.

2 · The old page itself is closed by JetBoost. Hiding the link doesn't make the page's own URL stop working. To have /sessions/{slug} actually turn into the "Session Expired" page after 72 hours, we use a small paid add-on called JetBoost.

JetBoost watches each session's expire date and retires the page when it passes, so anyone hitting an old link lands on the Session Expired page instead of stale content. This is a one-time setup for the site, handled by whoever administers Webflow:

  • Purchase JetBoost and connect it to the Darren Hardy LLC site.
  • Point its scheduling feature at the Sessions collection's expire date.
  • Set it to retire the item when that date passes, so Webflow serves the Expired page.
You, day to day

Once JetBoost is set up, you never touch it. Filling the sheet and publishing is all you do — the expire date you already entered drives everything. JetBoost is a set-it-once admin task, not part of the weekly routine.

Step 7 · Right-column cards

The promo cards beside each session

The right-hand column of every session has two things that change per session — the Moneyline quote and the Featured CTA — plus a stack of promo cards below them (the store, the Journal, program promos, and so on). Those promo cards live in their own list so you can add, remove, and reorder them without touching any session.

1

They're one reorderable collection

Every promo card is an item in the Sidebar Cards collection. Each has an Order number — lower shows higher. To reorder, change the numbers. To hide one, flip its Active switch off. No design work.

2

Every card is fully editable

Image, link (the image and the button share the same link), eyebrow, headline, description, and button text — all yours to set per card. Leave the button text blank for a card with no button.

3

Program cards can be smart

A card meant for a specific audience (like the Champion program) can carry a HubSpot CTA code instead of a fixed image, so HubSpot decides what that person sees. Add the CTA code to the card's CTA ID field; leave it blank for a normal card.

How to change them

For occasional changes, edit the Sidebar Cards collection directly in Webflow, or add a Sidebar Cards tab to your intake sheet and let Claude update them in the same run. You only list the cards you're changing — everything else stays as-is. The Moneyline and Featured CTA are not in this list; those ride along with each session row, since they change every day.

Step 8 · Good to know

Gotchas & FAQ

I keep getting logged out of Webflow when a teammate works on it.

That's a shared-seat thing, not a mistake on your end. Do your building and reviewing in Claude (which uses the connector, not your live Designer login), and only jump into Webflow for the one full-site Publish. If seats are a recurring problem, ask about a free reviewer seat for whoever publishes.

Do the pages update on their own, or do I have to publish every day?

On their own. After one publish per batch, sessions reveal on their Published date and drop off 72 hours later automatically. You only publish again to load the next batch. This is why loading a ~30-day batch at once is safe.

Can someone reach a future session early by guessing the link?

Technically yes — because all items in a batch are published up front, a future session's page exists before its day (the feeds just hide the link until then). For daily content that's fine. If we ever need future pages truly unreachable until their date, JetBoost can also handle scheduled publishing; that's an admin decision, not part of this routine.

What if one row is wrong or missing something?

Claude keeps going and flags that row in the review list with the reason, rather than stopping the whole batch. Fix that one cell and re-run just that row.

Do I ever need to open the Webflow Designer or write code?

No. Filling the sheet, sending it to Claude, reviewing links, and clicking Publish is the entire job. The only Designer touch is the one Publish button.

Where do the CTA codes come from?

They're HubSpot CTA IDs (long codes like c31dd636-…). You can copy one from the matching page on dd.darrenhardy.com or from HubSpot. Paste just the code into the Featured CTA ID column. Blank uses the default. The CTA image itself lives in HubSpot, so editing it there updates every session with no re-upload.
