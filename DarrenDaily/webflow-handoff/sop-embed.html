How to publish a DarrenDaily Session.

There are two ways to get a session onto the site. Your team picks the one that fits. Both are laid out below, along with the one-time setup you do first.

Pick your method

Two ways to do this

Same result either way: each session becomes a live /sessions/{slug} page. The difference is how you get there. This is a team decision, so here are both, plain and side by side.

Option A

Spreadsheet + Claude

Keep a running list of sessions in a shared spreadsheet. When it's ready, hand the sheet to Claude and it builds the pages for you in one pass.

  • AI-powered: saves hours of hands-on work and man-power per batch
  • One shared sheet, always up to date
  • A teammate can pre-review rows before they go
  • Built-in #OCE checker: flags likely typos and grammar on import for you to approve, never silently fixed
  • Good for loading many sessions at once
See the steps ↓
Option B

By hand in Webflow

Fill the same intake sheet to prep, then type each session into the Webflow CMS by hand instead of handing it to Claude.

  • Quick start: 7 simple steps →
  • Same prep sheet, no connector needed
  • Familiar if you already know Webflow
  • Best for one or two sessions at a time

Before you start

One-time setup

Do these once. After that you skip straight to whichever option you chose.

The short checklist

  1. Set up JetBoost on the site. This is what actually closes an expired session page. One-time, done by whoever administers Webflow.Details in step 1 below.
  2. Connect the Webflow connector in Claude. Only needed for Option A (spreadsheet + Claude).Skip if you're doing everything by hand.
  3. Download the intake spreadsheet and save it to the team Google Drive. That shared copy becomes your running list.Download button is in Option A, step 2.
1

Set up JetBoost (page expiry)

Every DarrenDaily session lives 72 hours, then its page should close and send anyone on an old link to the "Session Expired" page. That closing is handled by a small paid add-on called JetBoost. It's a one-time setup for the whole site, done by whoever administers Webflow:

  • Purchase JetBoost and connect it to the Darren Hardy LLC site.
  • Point its scheduling feature at the Sessions collection's Expire date.
  • Set it to retire the item when that date passes, so Webflow serves the Expired page.

Once this is set, nobody touches it again. The Expire date on each session drives it automatically.

2

Connect Webflow in Claude (Option A only)

This is the bridge that lets Claude build pages on our site. In Claude's settings, open Connectors, find Webflow, and authorize it against the Darren Hardy LLC workspace. You use the normal Claude app or claude.ai for all of this; there's nothing to install and you never touch code.

3

Put the spreadsheet in Google Drive (Option A only)

Download the intake sheet (button in Option A) and upload it to the team Google Drive as your master copy. Keeping it in Drive gives you two things:

  • A running list. Everyone adds upcoming sessions to the same sheet, so there's always one source of truth.
  • A pre-review step. Before a batch goes to Claude, a teammate can open the sheet and check the rows: titles, dates, and captions.

When a batch is approved, hand that sheet (or the approved rows) to Claude in step 3 of Option A.

Option A · Spreadsheet + Claude

Fill a sheet, hand it to Claude

You fill one row per session in a shared spreadsheet, then send it to Claude with the bulk instructions file. Claude builds every page, sets the dates, optimizes the images, and hands you links to review.

1

Download the sheet, keep it in Drive

Grab the template and upload it to the team Google Drive as the running master list (see setup step 3). The two example rows at the top are real, already-published sessions; leave them as a reference or delete them before sending.

Downloads Sessions-Intake-Template.csv. Open it in Excel, or import it into Google Sheets and share that with the team.

2

Add one row per session

If the session already lives on dd.darrenhardy.com, you usually only need two cells filled: the source URL and the published date. Claude pulls the rest from that page.

Brand-new session (no source page)?

Fill the three required columns yourself: Name, Published Date (as 2026-07-28), and Vimeo ID (the number from vimeo.com/1210284296). Then add the caption and meta description in their columns.

What each column is for

ColumnWhat to put there
NameThe session title, exactly as it should read.
Published DateYYYY-MM-DD. Drives the date on the page, the sort order, and the 72-hour expiry.
Vimeo IDThe number only, e.g. 1210284296.
CaptionThe comment prompt under the video. Wrap the lead-in in **double asterisks** for bold.
Secondary CaptionThe follow-up / referral copy under the caption.
Meta DescriptionAbout 155 characters for search and social previews.
Banner columnsOptional per-session banners under the video or pinned on top. Blank uses the site defaults.
Expire DateAuto. Leave blank. See the note just below.
Other AUTO columnsSlug, thumbnail, social image, SEO title. Filled for you.
Why you don't type an expire date

Every session lives exactly 72 hours, so the expire date is just the Published date plus 72 hours. The sheet fills it in for you, which is why you leave that column blank.

That one date does two jobs: the feeds use it to drop the session from every list and exit-popup after 72 hours, and JetBoost uses it to close the actual page (turn the URL into the Session Expired page). If you ever need a different window, type your own date in the Expire column to override.

What time they go live and expire, and the timezone

Publish and expire are exact times, not just dates, and Webflow stores them in UTC. JetBoost closes the page on that same expire time.

The convention: each session goes live at 13:00 UTC on its own date (about 6:00 AM Pacific / 9:00 AM Eastern, the morning it is meant to land), and expires exactly 72 hours later (13:00 UTC three days on). If you enter only a date, the automation sets this 13:00 UTC time for you.

Heads up on the display: the Webflow Designer shows these times in the workspace timezone (US Pacific), not UTC, so 13:00 UTC reads as about 6:00 AM Pacific on the session's date. When in doubt, match the existing sessions: they all publish at 13:00 UTC on their label date.

Images: paste a public link

For any image, paste a link that anyone can open (not a private file). Good sources:

  • A Google Drive share link set to "Anyone with the link"
  • The image's address on dd.darrenhardy.com (right-click the image, Copy image address)
  • A Dropbox or other public share link
  • Nothing at all for the thumbnail: the Vimeo poster is used automatically

Any format is fine (JPG, PNG). Every image that shows on the page is auto-converted to fast WebP on upload. The one exception is the Social Share image, kept as PNG/JPG on purpose because some Facebook and LinkedIn previews break on WebP.

Target sizes (also shown in the sheet column headers): thumbnail 1280 x 720, inline banner 1720 x 464, sticky banner 1600 x 200, social share 1200 x 630.

The sheet is evergreen: no duplicates

Because your Google Drive sheet keeps growing, the prompt tells Claude to check the collection first and only add sessions that aren't already there. So you can leave old rows in place, keep appending new ones, and re-run any time without creating duplicates. Claude reports which rows it skipped as already built.

The 30-day batch window

You can safely load up to about 30 days of sessions ahead in one batch. Each reveals itself on its Published date and drops off the feeds 72 hours later on its own. Load the batch, publish once (step 5), and walk away. Keep each batch inside about 30 days so the earliest upcoming post doesn't get pushed out of the "latest sessions" window.

Rough time: by hand runs about 20 to 30 minutes per session; a full batch through Claude is one prompt of about five minutes plus your review. That trade is why this option exists.

4

Review the links it hands back

When the batch finishes, Claude posts a table of every session it built, each with a clickable link. Open them and spot-check. Anything that needed a fallback or hit a snag is flagged with the reason.

It also flags copy issues. Claude reads the copy for typos, grammar, and confusing or ambiguous phrasing (a line can be spelled perfectly and still read two ways). Each one comes back in a simple flags table, one row per issue, with a plain-English flag and a suggested rewrite for you to approve or ignore in a glance. It never changes Darren's wording on its own, so nothing is silently rewritten.

If a task manager is connected

If something like Asana is connected to your Claude session, it can also push a review task with those links straight to whoever does the checking, so the reviewer gets pinged instead of relying on someone spotting the chat. Just ask for it in the same message.

5

Publish

Claude publishes each session item as it goes. To refresh the cross-page "latest sessions" strips and make brand-new pages fully live, one person opens Webflow and clicks the big Publish (top-right). One publish per batch. After that the site self-rotates for the whole window, so you don't re-publish to reveal or expire anything.

Claude will prompt you to hit Publish whenever it's needed, so this step never gets missed.

Can the assisted path "Schedule to publish later"?

Not natively: that's a Designer feature, and the tools Claude uses publish immediately, so Claude can't set a timed auto-publish. It doesn't need to for the feeds: the built-in date filter already keeps every future session hidden from the lists and exit-pops until its Published date, and JetBoost expires the page at 72 hours. The one difference from native scheduling is that a future session's page is reachable by direct URL before its day (it just isn't linked anywhere). If you want those pages hidden-until-date too, have Claude create the future sessions as drafts, then use "Schedule to publish later" on them in the Designer.

Option B · By hand in Webflow

Build each session in the CMS

You still fill the intake spreadsheet first, so every field is prepped in one place. The difference from Option A is that you type the rows into Webflow yourself instead of handing the sheet to Claude. Good if you already know the CMS, or for one or two sessions at a time.

Quick start: a new session in 7 steps

  1. Log in to Webflow.
  2. Open the DarrenDaily site, then the CMS.
  3. Click the blue "+ New Session" button.
  4. Fill in all the needed fieldsWhat each field is: see the steps below.
  5. Click the blue down-arrow next to "Create draft."
  6. Choose the publishing typePublish now, Queue for next site publish, or Schedule to publish later.
  7. View and check it via its URL.

The steps below spell out each one in detail.

1

Fill the intake sheet first (your prep)

Use the same Sessions intake spreadsheet (download button in Option A, or the team's Google Drive copy) and fill one row per session. It's your checklist so nothing gets missed: title, dates, Vimeo ID, caption, meta description, and image links at the sizes shown in the headers. Then copy each value into Webflow in the next steps.

2

Open the Sessions collection

In Webflow, open the CMS panel (the left sidebar), choose the Sessions collection, and click the blue New Session button (top right).

3

Fill the fields from your sheet

Copy each value from your prepped row into the matching field. The important ones:

Set the Expire date yourself

In the manual route there's no auto-fill, so type the Expire date as the Published date plus 72 hours. Set both as UTC times, exactly as in Option A: publish at 13:00 UTC on the session's date (about 6:00 AM Pacific), and expire at 13:00 UTC three days later. That timestamp is what the feeds and JetBoost both read to retire the session, so don't skip it.

4

Add the images

Upload the thumbnail (and any banner images) into the item's image fields. Webflow automatically serves uploaded images in optimized formats including WebP, so a normal JPG or PNG is fine. Leave the Social Share image as PNG/JPG on purpose. If you left the thumbnail blank, use the Vimeo poster image for that video.

5

Preview it before you publish

With the session open, click the eye icon at the top right ("View content on Session page") to see the finished page exactly as visitors will, your content flowed into the template. Check the video and caption, then come back to publish.

6

Publish, or schedule it

The item's Create draft button has a down-arrow that opens a dropdown with three choices: Publish now, Queue for next site publish, and Schedule to publish later.

For a session dated ahead, use Schedule to publish later and set the go-live to the session's publish moment (13:00 UTC, about 6:00 AM Pacific, on the session's date). Webflow keeps the page unpublished and unreachable until then, then publishes it for you automatically. That's the cleanest way to load ahead without a future page being reachable early. JetBoost still closes the page once the Expire date passes.

The cross-page "latest sessions" strips refresh on a site publish; if a scheduled session hasn't shown up in them yet, a quick site Publish updates them.

Good to know

Gotchas & FAQ

Can I edit a session that's already live?

Yes, anytime, and it won't break anything. Open the session (either path), change what you need, and hit Publish again so the edit goes live. Then always open the page and check it yourself afterward, especially if the session is already getting traffic, so you can confirm the change looks right to visitors before you walk away.

Do the pages update on their own, or do I publish every day?

On their own. After one publish, sessions reveal on their Published date and drop off the feeds 72 hours later automatically. You only publish again to load the next batch (Option A) or add the next session (Option B).

What exactly does JetBoost do, versus the feeds?

Two different jobs. The feeds hide the session's link from every list and exit-popup after 72 hours (built in, no plugin). JetBoost closes the actual page so an old /sessions/{slug} link turns into the Session Expired page. Both read the same Expire date.

I keep getting logged out of Webflow when a teammate is working.

That's a shared-seat thing. With Option A, most of the work happens in Claude (through the connector, not your live Designer login), and you only open Webflow for the one Publish. If seats are a recurring problem, ask about a reviewer seat for whoever publishes.

Can someone reach a future session early by guessing the link?

Technically yes, because items in a batch are published up front, so a future page exists before its day (the feeds just hide the link until then). Fine for daily content. If future pages ever need to be truly unreachable until their date, JetBoost can also do scheduled publishing; that's an admin decision.
